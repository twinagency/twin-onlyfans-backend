 01 / DEVELOPER CALL BRIEF


 OnlyFans CRM Commercial readiness
 31 October 2026 · Brisbane time · Read-only production and source audit

 Decision: not ready to promise the requested capacity. The system can send replies and paid
 media today, but the running account-wide send gate mathematically prevents 10,000 chatting fans
 per creator per day. A larger server alone cannot remove that limit.

 Open the call with this
 “We need to support creators with 100,000 fans and around 10,000 chatting each day. Different fans
 must be handled concurrently, with an intentional delay of at least 25 seconds and a response within
 three minutes. We also need reliable paid-media delivery, purchase recognition and human takeover.
 Please explain the current bottlenecks, which are inherited from FASTT, which are our additions, and
 prove the supported throughput on the actual production build.”

 The seven decisions to get from the developer
 • Account throughput: why is there a hard 20-second gap between sends across an entire creator
   account, and what provider-tested limit can replace it?

 • Parallel work: how will we move beyond four preparation tasks per account and the default
   64-account supervisor limit?

 • Timing: which timestamp starts the 25-180 second clock, and how are delayed, skipped and
   unanswered turns measured?

 • Content sales: can one controlled scenario prove conversation assessment, correct media and
   price, provider receipt, payment, unlock and purchase memory?

 • Imports: how will new messages, purchases, history and vault imports all progress without starving
   one another?

 • Operator control: can a chatter see exactly who AI owns, interrupt a pending send, and hand back
   safely? What is actually enforced when Infloww is also used?

 • Release ownership: who owns one reproducible source release, its regression checks, monitoring
   and rollback?

 A concrete example that exposes the problem
 Twenty different fans message the same creator at once. At the current 20-second account-wide gap,
 the twentieth first reply cannot start until at least 380 seconds after the first send. If the first reply
 waits 25 seconds, the twentieth is no earlier than 405 seconds, or 6 minutes 45 seconds. That already
 fails the three-minute requirement, without needing a 100,000-fan test.
 Audit scope: inspected live AWS containers, resource settings, runtime source, database aggregates, recent logs,
 stored test reports, relevant GitHub changes and upstream FASTT source. No settings were changed, no services
 restarted, and no fan messages or purchases were sent. This is an evidence-based diagnosis, not load or security
 certification.

 02 / WORKLOAD AND ARCHITECTURE


 Define the capacity correctly
 Assumption for planning: 100,000 stored fans and 10,000 distinct chatting fans per creator per day.
 Daily active fans are not the same as simultaneous requests. Reply turns, message bubbles, media
 sends and peak arrivals must all be specified.

   AI replies per active            Replies per               Average                100 creators: replies/day
   fan/day                          creator/day               replies/second

   1                                10,000                    0.116                  1 million

   5                                50,000                    0.579                  5 million

   10                               100,000                   1.157                  10 million

   20                               200,000                   2.315                  20 million


 Current theoretical ceiling: one send every 20 seconds = 3/minute = 180/hour = 4,320/day per
 creator. This is an upper bound, not an achieved rate. Multiple bubbles, media cards, manual sends,
 failures and other overhead can reduce useful reply capacity further.

 At 10 replies per active fan, the average demand is about 23 times that ceiling. A planning burst of
 five times the daily average is 5.79 reply turns/second per creator, or 579/second across 100
 creators. The burst factor is a proposed test assumption, not measured customer behaviour.

 What the running code does
 • Generation: FASTT_PREPARE_CONCURRENCY defaults to 4 per account and is clamped to 16. Logs
   confirm four in-flight tasks. Raising it alone does not remove the send gate.

 • Sending: native_pilot/tuning.py enforces a 20-second floor. native_pilot/send_gate.py serializes the
   wire path per account. The database shares next_dispatch_at with other sends.

 • Accounts: the supervisor defaults to 64 eligible accounts, with a configurable ceiling of 256. A
   default deployment cannot serve a 100-creator fleet without changes and resource validation.

 • Budgets: the current AI sender uses a rolling 2,000-per-fan daily allowance. Do not confuse legacy
   account limit_units fields with the effective AI limit. Model cost caps are a separate constraint.

 Translate “no queues” into an enforceable requirement
 Use concurrent work across independent fans, ordered work within each fan, and no sustained
 backlog inside the agreed load envelope. A durable internal scheduler is still necessary for the
 25-second delay, restart recovery and duplicate protection. It must not make one fan wait for another
 fan’s full conversation.

 Retain creator-wide provider rate limits, but base them on verified throughput and provider
 behaviour. No evidence found establishes the hard-coded 20-second rule as an OnlyFans-mandated
 limit. Do not simply remove it and assume unlimited sends are safe.
 Implementation options include a durable job store with short claims or a message broker partitioned by tenant +
 creator + fan. AWS documents parallel FIFO processing across message groups; PostgreSQL documents SKIP LOCKED
 for queue-like consumers. Neither mechanism bypasses OnlyFans or model-provider limits.

 05 / CONTENT, PURCHASES AND MEMORY


 Make media sales and onboarding
 dependable
 There is some paid delivery, but the entire sales loop is not proven
 In the 24-hour receipt-matched subset, Maile had 109 sent messages including 3 paid media cards;
 Alva had 93 messages including 1 paid card. No purchased marker was present on those four cards in
 the inspected message rows. This is not a conversion-rate estimate or proof that no purchases
 occurred elsewhere.

 The new conversation evaluator is enabled and running. In a bounded two-hour log sample, 38
 evaluations ended in the combined reason no_supported_interest_or_evaluation_failed; two
 further opportunities were withheld for stale purchase state. No recent row was found in
 of_contextual_offer. These observations do not prove that every rejected conversation should have
 received an offer, but they do mean we cannot yet claim successful proactive selling from this path.

 • Separate a legitimate “keep chatting” decision from provider errors, parsing failures, missing
   evidence, unmatched content and stale payment data. The current combined reason hides the
   distinction.

 • Prove one controlled offer from engagement to matching content, correct price/caption, delivery
   receipt, payment, unlock, owned-media exclusion and subsequent follow-up.

 Description coverage remains incomplete
 Counts below mean a nonempty description or video_description on an active vault item. They do not prove accuracy
 or approval, and some metadata may exist elsewhere.

   Creator                  Photos described / stored                    Videos described / stored

   Maile                    367 / 524                                    4 / 26

   Alva                     1,138 / 1,769                                17 / 812

   Naomi                    1,895 / 3,020                                32 / 770

   Emma                     0 / 420                                      0 / 16


 • Import every eligible folder/page, verify media ownership and playable assets, describe content,
   attach approved prices and boundaries, and measure sale-ready inventory. Import count alone is
   not readiness.
 • Preserve exact manually selected attachments, previews, caption and price. If the user requests a
   different item, evaluate it independently; do not force purchase of the earlier card. Reuse an
   existing card for the same item and stop chasing ignored offers.

 • Do not announce that content has been sent until there is a confirmed provider result. On an
   uncertain response, reconcile before any retry that might duplicate the paid offer.

 Onboarding needs explicit completion gates
 Emma’s vault is progressing, but every inspected launch-history scan still showed needed, with no
 completion timestamp. Amelia and Cristina had not completed purchase scans. Show separate
 progress and retryable reasons for connection, fans, history, vault, descriptions, purchases and
 activation.

 History-memory is active. A separate profile-learning pipeline is overdue; consolidate ownership and
 expose freshness. Preserve the intentional new-fans-off rule.

 06 / CONTROLS AND RELIABILITY


 Agency operations must be safe and
 observable
 One effective owner per conversation
 • Display AI active, waiting to send, human controlled, paused and failed with concrete reasons and
   next actions. An “automated” flag alone does not show that a reply can currently be sent.

 • Human takeover must cancel queued or generating AI work, check authority immediately before
   transport, and preserve any send already accepted by the provider. Hand-back must not replay old
   questions or duplicate pending cards.

 • Native OnlyFans ownership-list syncing is a single-fan Maile pilot in PR 70. Its list membership
   cannot prevent a chatter sending in Infloww. Verify external refresh, routing, handover delay and
   the policy for conflicting sends before promising coexistence.

 • Bulk enable/disable must be resumable, scoped, revision-checked and explicit about skipped fans.
   Existing chats and newly arriving fans are different populations; honour the current new-fans-off
   rule.

 Handle failures without silence or duplicate sends
 The live work board contained one Maile unresolved-delivery hold, frozen-dispatch-unavailable. A
 two-hour sample logged that reconciliation reason 226 times. It also logged 1,146
 reply-schedule-terminal holds and repeated hold/resume transitions. These are event counts, not
 distinct fan failures. Trace the affected durable states, stop unproductive retries, and make
 unresolved outcomes actionable.

 • Every eligible turn needs a durable outcome: sent with provider ID, explicitly skipped, waiting with
   deadline, human-owned, or failed with recovery. An empty live queue is not proof that every input
   was answered.

 • Retry model and network failures with bounded backoff and budgets. A timeout after a provider
   accepted a card must not trigger a second sale. Use one-use intents, receipt reconciliation and
   per-fan locking.
 • Instrument provider arrival, ingestion, model start/end, eligibility time, wire start and receipt. Track
   earliest unanswered age, deadline misses, lost inputs, duplicates, offer funnel, stale purchases and
   recovery time per creator.

 Scale the agency-facing system too
 • Use server-side pagination and indexed search for 100,000-fan rosters. Test bulk actions, mobile
   composer, attachments, price, captions and refresh at that size.

 • Prove tenant/agency isolation across API routes, jobs, media URLs, caches, exports and staff
   permissions. Verify that a staff member cannot read or operate another agency’s creator. No
   cross-tenant breach is asserted by this audit.

 • Separate model/provider limits, live reply resources, media analysis and bulk imports. Test global
   token budgets and fair sharing so one large creator cannot consume the fleet.

 • Treat host loss, database restore and service restart as release tests. Current core services share
   one host; failover, off-host restore and recovery objectives were not demonstrated here.

 Release blocker: production overlays must become one reviewed, reproducible release. Test exact
 assembled images and preserve newer fixes during rollback. Existing fixture failures are not a green
 acceptance result.

 07 / DEVELOPER ACCEPTANCE PLAN


 What must pass before selling this
 capacity
 Proposed baseline: 100,000 stored fans per creator, 10,000 daily chatting fans, 10 reply turns per
 active fan, measured with a five-times-average peak. Confirm the desired creator count, active hours
 and extra message bubbles before committing to a commercial limit.

   Test                       Required evidence / pass condition

   Full-size dataset          1, 10 and 100 creators; up to 10 million fan records at fleet scale. Inbox, filters, bulk
                              controls and imports remain responsive. Suggested UI target: p95 below 2 seconds for
                              paginated inbox/search.

   Concurrent live-turn       100,000 reply turns/day per creator, then five-times-average peaks. Use realistic
   simulation                 model latency, token sizes, media preparation and provider responses. Test the actual
                              assembled API, worker, sender and database.

   Burst example              1,000 fans message one creator in the same minute while history and vault imports
                              run. All eligible first replies must meet the 180-second deadline, with no duplicates or
                              unaccounted inputs.

   Timing                     Agree first unanswered message / grouped input clock. Enforce no earlier than 25
                              seconds from the agreed anchor. Every in-scope response must be within 180 seconds
                              in acceptance; measure p50, p95, p99, max and unanswered. Aim below the ceiling to
                              leave headroom.

   Isolation and takeover     A slow creator, human takeover, paused fan or changed account session must not stall
                              other creators or leak content. Zero unauthorized or post-takeover sends in the test.

   Sales lifecycle            Explicit request and proactive interest both tested; changed request, ignored offer,
                              already-owned media, insufficient catalogue, manual caption/price, purchase/unlock
                              and follow-up all verified.

   Faults and recovery        Inject model timeout, invalid JSON, 429, expired session, proxy failure, lost receipt,
                              duplicate/out-of-order event and crash during send. Verify durable recovery without
                              duplicate charges or false sent status.

   Duration and rollout       24-hour mixed-load soak including a deployment and restart. Then a controlled
                              provider-backed canary to verify actual connection limits. Expand in measured cohorts
                              with rollback and monitoring.



 Priority and ownership
 • P0, backend/runtime owner: reconcile the exact live source, settle the provider throughput
   budget, remove the architectural account bottleneck through a tested design, and prove the 25-180
   second target.

 • P0, commerce/integration owner: prove contextual paid delivery, purchase/unlock
   reconciliation, usable media coverage and trustworthy manual sends; resolve uncertain deliveries.

 • P1, platform/data owner: fair imports, memory freshness, database/pool sizing, isolation,
   monitoring, recovery and one repeatable release process.

 • P1, frontend/agency operations owner: actionable statuses, dependable takeover, large-roster
   performance, bulk progress and completed onboarding.
 Define the supported load and alert on provider outages, disconnected accounts and human pauses. Extra servers
 cannot guarantee provider availability. Use synthetic traffic for stress tests, not real fans.
